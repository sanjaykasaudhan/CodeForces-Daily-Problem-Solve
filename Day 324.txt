🛠️ The Approach The problem requires us to build a permutation of numbers from 1 to n split into two equal halves, where the minimum of the left half is exactly a and the maximum of the right half is exactly b. 
Fast I/O amp; Initialization: I instantly loaded the data using sys.stdin.read().split() and seeded my two halves with their required boundaries: left = [a] and right = [b]. 
The Greedy Descending Loop: To ensure a remains the absolute minimum on the left, we should pack the left half with the largest available numbers. Conversely, the right half should get the smallest remaining numbers so b stays the maximum. I achieved this by iterating backwards using for i in range(n, 0, -1):. 
Targeted Population: Inside the loop, I skipped our seed values using if i == a or i == b: continue. I then greedily filled the left array until it reached half capacity (if len(left) lt; n // 2:), pushing all remaining smaller numbers into the right array. 
Final Validation: Instead of writing complex pre-checks, I just validated the final constructed halves: if min(left) == a and max(right) == b:. If valid, I joined and printed them; otherwise, I outputted -1. 

⏱️ Complexity Analysis
Time Complexity: O(N) per testcase. Constructing the arrays via the descending loop and validating them with Pythons built-in min() and max() functions both require a rapid linear scan. 
Space/Memory Complexity: O(N) auxiliary space per testcase. Memory is strictly allocated to buffer the dynamically sized left and right arrays and the output strings before printing to standard output.
