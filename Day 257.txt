🚀 Codeforces Problem Solved: farmpiggie and Subset Sum

I recently solved Problem A — farmpiggie and Subset Sum from Codeforces Round 1108 (Div. 2). The problem is a great example of how a simple parity observation can turn a seemingly complex subset-sum problem into a straightforward construction.

🔗Problem Link: https://lnkd.in/g4e2Uve7

🧠 Problem Idea
We are given an even integer n and need to construct a permutation p of 1...n.
For every position i, we can choose:
-i × p[i]
0
+i × p[i]
The goal is to construct the permutation so that no matter which choices are made, the final sum can never be ****1.

💡 Key Observation: Think About Parity!
Instead of trying to control every possible sum, we only need to make sure that every possible contribution is even.
If every value:
i × p[i]
is even, then any combination of:
±(i × p[i]) or 0
will also produce an even final sum.
And an even number can never be 1. 🎯

🔑 Construction
Start with the identity permutation:
1 2 3 4 5 6 ...
Then swap every adjacent pair:
1 2 3 4 5 6
↓
2 1 4 3 6 5
So the permutation becomes:
[2, 1, 4, 3, 6, 5, ...]
Now observe:
Odd position → gets an even number
Even position → gets an odd number
Therefore:
odd × even = even
even × odd = even
So every i × p[i] is even.
Hence, regardless of which operations are chosen, the final value is always even:
c_final ≠ 1

🔍 Example
For:
n = 6
Initially:
1 2 3 4 5 6
After swapping adjacent pairs:
2 1 4 3 6 5
Now:
1 × 2 = 2
2 × 1 = 2
3 × 4 = 12
4 × 3 = 12
5 × 6 = 30
6 × 5 = 30
Every value is even.
Therefore, any sum formed using positive, negative, or zero contributions will also be even.
So obtaining:
c_final = 1
is impossible.

⏱️ Complexity
For each test case:
Creating the permutation → O(n)
Swapping adjacent elements → O(n)
Printing the permutation → O(n)
Time Complexity: O(n)
Space Complexity: O(n)

🎯 Key Takeaway
When a problem asks you to avoid one particular value, look for an invariant that makes that value impossible.
Here, the invariant is parity.
Instead of considering exponentially many possible combinations of operations, we make every possible contribution even. That immediately eliminates 1 from the set of possible final sums.
This was a great reminder that in competitive programming:
Observation > Brute Force 🔥
