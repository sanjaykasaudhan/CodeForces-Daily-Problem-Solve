🛠️ The Approach The problem requires us to draw two perpendicular segments of at least length K within an X \times Y grid. Instead of calculating arbitrary slopes (which can lead to messy floating-point issues or out-of-bounds errors), I relied on a fundamental property of squares: their diagonals are always perfectly perpendicular.

Fast I/O: I used sys.stdin.read().split() to ingest all test cases instantly, bypassing Python's standard I/O overhead.

The Maximum Square: To ensure the segments are as long as possible to easily satisfy the K constraint, I found the largest square that can perfectly fit inside the given X \times Y boundaries. This is bounded by the smaller of the two dimensions: S = min(X, Y).

Coordinate Mapping: Once I had the square size S, placing the perpendicular segments was trivial. The first segment is the main diagonal running from the origin to the top-right corner: f"0 0 {S} {S}". The second segment is the anti-diagonal running from the y-axis to the x-axis: f"0 {S} {S} 0".

Batch Output: Both coordinates are dynamically formatted using f-strings, appended to the out array, and flushed via sys.stdout.write in a single operation.

⏱️ Complexity Analysis
Time Complexity: O(1) per testcase. The solution completely bypasses loop simulations, relying entirely on constant-time mathematical assignments. The overall time for t test cases scales linearly as O(t), executing in a lightning-fast 15 ms.

Space/Memory Complexity: O(1) auxiliary space per test case. We only allocate memory for the final output array before batch-printing.
